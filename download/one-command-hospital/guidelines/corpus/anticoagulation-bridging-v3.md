# Peri-operative Anticoagulation Bridging Protocol

> **SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE PROTOCOL** — authored to exercise the Guideline Copilot pipeline.
> Not medical advice. Not for clinical use.

- Corpus ID: ANTICOAG-BRIDGE | Edition: v3.0 | Effective: 2026-03-01
- Owner (synthetic): Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee

## 1. Scope

This protocol applies to adult patients on chronic warfarin therapy presenting for planned
elective surgery or invasive procedures. It does not cover direct oral anticoagulants
(DOACs), which are managed under a separate institutional protocol, and it does not cover
emergency surgery, where time-critical reversal pathways apply instead. Paediatric
anticoagulation is out of scope and must be discussed with the paediatric cardiology service.

## 2. Thromboembolic risk assessment

Classify every patient before planning interruption:

- **High risk:** mechanical mitral valve; stroke, TIA, or systemic embolism within the
  previous 3 months; rheumatic valvular disease with atrial fibrillation.
- **Moderate risk:** mechanical aortic valve with an additional risk factor; venous
  thromboembolism (VTE) within the previous 3–6 months; multiple moderate factors.
- **Low risk:** atrial fibrillation without high-risk features; mechanical bileaflet
  aortic valve without additional risk factors; single prior VTE more than 12 months ago.

## 3. Pre-operative bridging

- **Low risk:** interrupt warfarin 5 days before the procedure. **No bridging is required.**
- **High risk:** interrupt warfarin 5 days before the procedure; begin therapeutic-dose
  low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) 3 days before; the **last LMWH dose is given
  approximately 24 hours before** the procedure.
- **Moderate risk:** decide case-by-case; bridging may be considered after an individual
  risk review documented in the record.

## 4. Day-of-procedure checks

Confirm coagulation control during interruption: check INR 1–2 days before the procedure.
The procedure proceeds only if **INR < 1.5**. If INR is ≥ 1.5 on the day before surgery,
give **oral vitamin K 1–2.5 mg** and recheck.

## 5. Post-operative resumption

- **Minor procedures with adequate haemostasis:** resume warfarin at the usual evening
  dose **12–24 hours** after the procedure.
- **High-bleed-risk procedures:** delay therapeutic anticoagulation **48–72 hours**;
  resumption requires documented haemostasis. In the interim, prophylactic-dose LMWH may
  begin 24 hours after surgery if surgical haemostasis is secure.
- **Neuraxial catheters:** follow the institutional neuraxial timing guidance before
  giving LMWH after epidural or spinal catheter removal.

## 6. Special situations

- **Mechanical mitral valve:** always classify as high risk; **bridging is required**
  per Section 3.
- **VTE within the previous 3 months:** consider deferring elective surgery; if surgery
  cannot be deferred, involve haematology and discuss inferior vena cava filter placement
  in the peri-operative plan.

## 7. Escalation and contacts

Uncertainty at any step: contact the anticoagulation stewardship pharmacist. Mechanical
valve patients with complex histories are discussed with cardiology before interruption.
Any deviation from this protocol must be documented with rationale.
